# After the objective function — noticing a tendency, MHQ, and two books

On 4 September I published a Japanese essay, [Rewriting the objective function — change management in the well-being era, and the ground under Japanese youth](https://note.com/koheinoda/n/nbbf6602c4344). An English version of that piece is ready to go up with this one.

The point still stands. The hardness of life for young people in Japan is not “personal weakness.” What we should face is not a charge against their “hearts,” but the objective function of the organisations and the society around them. When the operating logic is the maximisation of wealth and status, it is difficult to secure well-being at one’s feet and then move toward a better future.

The essay stopped there. Refusing to blame the heart is not the same as refusing to notice the state of the heart. If we leave the objective function entirely on the social side, and look at nothing on the personal side, that is another bias.

My own starting point is the cognitive science of emotion, and research on depression. In 2009 I tried a workplace depression-risk questionnaire; MHQ, the mental health questionnaire, grew from that line of work. After I returned to Japan at the end of 2020, through 2022 and 2023 I tried, as far as I could, to put that questionnaire back into the world and to work on well-being inside organisations as an HR practitioner. As I wrote in that essay, the situation inside organisations stayed grim where it was grim, and I myself had to step off the front line for a time.

So this sequel is not a sales note. It is the link between that essay’s “social objective function” and the “personal tendency” I have worked with for a long time.

## What it measures is not a disease name

MHQ (the public version now is MHQ2) is a questionnaire of 120 items, about 12–15 minutes. In the range we publish, it touches stress environment, stress situation, social support, depression risk, and the communication domain. Companies use it in hiring, internal review, and development. Individuals can take it too.

I want this to be clear. It is not for confirming a diagnosis. The numbers are not disease names. It is a questionnaire for noticing your own tendencies and turning attention toward what to improve. We should not take a worsening trend in youth statistics and turn it into a hunt for an individual diagnosis. As I wrote there, this is also a problem of social soil.

Even so, it is shaky to discuss only the soil and never look at one’s own state. In leadership development I have seen capable people wear down under pressure, many times. I wrote about multiple intelligences in [the earlier essay on updating the OS of learning](https://note.com/koheinoda/n/ne5355b32099c). A career that never grew the literacies of body, relationship, and inner work is especially brittle when the objective function stays on “maximise.”

A questionnaire does not rewrite society. It can, once, put outside the person where attention is going. The “freedom to regulate impulse” in humanistic management does not start as an abstraction. If you cannot see your own tendency, there is nothing to regulate.

## The two books are for after the questionnaire

In recent years I published two books on that continuation.

The first, for noticing a tendency, is *The book you should read when you think you would be depression* (2024).

[Amazon](https://www.amazon.co.jp/dp/B0DGFRYHMX)

After noticing, the book for how to attend to stress is *Stress Defense Strategies in the VUCA Era: Tips for Preventing Depression and Accumulating Stress* (Kohei Noda, Mame, Rie Kaishita, 2025).

[Amazon](https://www.amazon.co.jp/dp/B0DTS8XLPD)

The use can stay simple. See a tendency with the questionnaire. Read the books for where to put attention. For the detailed version, read it together in a feedback session if needed. This is not treatment. There is a 30-minute session and a 60-minute session.

They are not a special “youth edition.” That essay used youth statistics as a way into the operating logic of society. The questionnaire and the books are tools a person or an organisation can hold after that. A parent, a teacher, an HR lead, or the person themselves — any of those doors is fine.
